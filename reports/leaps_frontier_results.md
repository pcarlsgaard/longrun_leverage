# The LEAPS efficient frontier, and which of it survives being doubted

Generated by `letf.leaps_frontier`. Window 1986-09-29 to 2026-09-02, 10,059 sessions, on the calendar, financing and option assumptions of `reports/hedge_alternatives_results.md`.

Earlier work here picked three or four option structures by hand and asked whether
they survived. This asks where the frontier *is*. A prespecified regular lattice —
16 S&P structures (3 strikes x 5 premium budgets, plus the conservative
anchor SPX_80_25) and 28 Nasdaq structures (4 strikes x 7 budgets) — is carried
through one primary bootstrap and seven sensitivities, and a cell is called
efficient only if it is still efficient after the assumptions that produced it
are moved. No intermediate cell was added at any point, however well a
neighbour performed.

Everything else is inherited and fixed: roughly 2-year calls, the annual roll on
listed expiries, residual capital in long Treasuries at 1.00x, a trailing
realized-volatility proxy over the option's own horizon plus 3% of volatility
premium, the same Black-Scholes framework and the same 100bp spread. The premium
budget is the control variable; nothing targets a delta.

## The two uncertainties are not the same size

*Strategy uncertainty* is what the lattice and the bootstrap measure. *Model
uncertainty* is the price of the options, and this repository holds no long-dated
option price history, no skew surface and no bid/ask record for either index.

Each structure therefore carries its own measured CAGR-per-volatility-point,
and it is not a constant across the lattice: it runs from 0.28% for
NDX_80_20 to 1.39% for SPX_95_50, so one volatility point of
doubt is worth 5.0 times as much CAGR at the expensive end of the
lattice as at the cheap end. The prespecified comparison tolerance is
0.25% of CAGR, and not one of the 44 structures has a volatility point smaller than that, so **the binding limit on what can
be resolved is the option-price assumption rather than the Monte Carlo**. Every
pairwise verdict here widens its bar to whichever is larger.

## Primary Monte Carlo

15,000 paths on the joint 63-session moving-block bootstrap, at 10, 20 and 30 years,
one fixed seed, **the same simulated worlds for every strategy**. Whole daily
rows are drawn, so the two indices, the leveraged funds, the Treasury sleeve, the
funding rate and the dividend levels keep the relationships they had on the day;
what is destroyed is the order of days beyond one block. Every comparison against
a rival below is therefore paired path by path.

Percentiles marked `p90`/`p95` on drawdown are *severity* percentiles: the
drawdown only a tenth, or a twentieth, of paths are worse than.

## The two frontiers

Both are computed over the 44 lattice cells alone. The unlevered indices are
drawn on the figures for reference but are excluded from the domination test:
letting an unlevered index sit at the low-risk corner of a LEAPS frontier would
define the question away.

- **Growth frontier** — median 30-year CAGR against median max drawdown: `reports/leaps_frontier_growth.png`, panel A. Panel B is what each premium budget buys in delta-equivalent equity.
- **Robust-growth frontier** — fifth-percentile 30-year CAGR against P(max drawdown worse than 60%): `reports/leaps_frontier_robust.png`, panel A. Panel B is the median against the bad case.

Domination is epsilon-domination on the prespecified tolerances: 0.25% of CAGR,
2% of drawdown, 2% of tail probability. Two cells inside those are the
same cell.

## Classification

A cell is **robust** if it is non-dominated in the primary specification and in at
least 5 of the 7 sensitivities, **conditional** if it is efficient at
baseline but not in most sensitivities, and **dominated** otherwise. Frontier
membership is recomputed inside each sensitivity rather than inferred by
differencing against the primary run: the candidate is asked the same question
seven more times.

Two classifications, because there are two questions. `classification` draws the
frontier over all 44 cells and answers whether the choice of underlying
matters; `family_classification` draws it inside the cell's own family and
answers which structures are efficient given that the index has already been
chosen. A cell can be efficient in its family and dominated jointly, and that
gap is exactly what the Nasdaq comparison is about.

`neighbour_support` is the fraction of a cell's own lattice neighbours — one
notch in strike or budget — that are themselves non-dominated. A single efficient
cell surrounded by dominated ones is a numerical winner, not a regime. It is
scored against both classifications for the same reason both exist: an S&P cell
whose neighbourhood is dominated by *Nasdaq* cells scores zero joint support
however solid it is among its own kind, so `family_neighbour_support` is the
column to read when the index is already chosen. The off-grid anchor SPX_80_25
has no lattice neighbours and is left blank in both.

## Sensitivities

Seven prespecified arms, all at the 30-year horizon, which is the horizon the
frontier is defined at. Within a case every arm sees the *same* resampled worlds,
so an option-price or leverage or roll sensitivity is a paired within-world
difference; only the block-length arms cannot be paired, because the block length
is what selects the days. A matched 63-session arm at the same path count is run
beside them so a block result is never compared against a different path count.

## Conclusions

**1. What is the robust S&P LEAPS frontier?** Drawn inside the S&P family — the question an investor who has already chosen the index is asking — it is SPX_80_25, SPX_85_30, SPX_85_35, SPX_85_40, SPX_85_45 and SPX_85_50: 6 of the 16 S&P cells, each non-dominated by another S&P cell in the primary run and in at least 5 of the 7 sensitivities, with 1 conditional and 9 dominated. Against the Nasdaq cells as well, SPX_80_25 survives — 1 of 16 S&P cells.

**2. Is there a genuine middle-risk regime between 85/30 and 90/50?** Within the S&P family, yes: SPX_85_35 (14.41% median, 7.19% p5, -47.7% median drawdown, P(DD>60%) 10.7%), SPX_85_40 (15.24% median, 7.17% p5, -52.4% median drawdown, P(DD>60%) 21.8%) and SPX_85_45 (15.97% median, 6.93% p5, -57.1% median drawdown, P(DD>60%) 38.3%). The separation test used is the prespecified tolerance set — 0.25% of CAGR, 2% of drawdown, 2% of tail probability — widened to one volatility point wherever that is larger.

**3. Does 85/40 survive?** SPX_85_40 is *dominated* against the whole lattice, holding 0 of 7 sensitivities, and *robust frontier* within the S&P family, holding 7. Against 85/30 it gains +1.78% of median CAGR and adds +17.7% to P(DD>60%), a price of 9.9 points of tail per point of CAGR. That gain clears the option-pricing bar of 0.67%, so the extra return is real. What makes 85/40 dominated is not that the return is illusory but that NDX_85_30 offers comparable growth with a materially shallower median drawdown; no S&P cell beats it, so what dominates it is the underlying rather than the structure.

**4. Does 90/40 survive?** SPX_90_40 is *dominated* against the whole lattice, holding 0 of 7 sensitivities, and *dominated* within the S&P family, holding 1. As an elbow it is genuine: the step into it (35% to 40%) costs 19.7 points of tail per CAGR point and the step out of it (40% to 45%) costs 27.4. The price of the next notch rises there, which is what an elbow is.

**5. At what premium budget does marginal tail risk begin rising disproportionately?** NDX on the notch out of 30% (its price rises +7.5 points of tail per CAGR point over the notch before it, the largest increment on the ladder) and SP500 on the notch out of 35% (its price rises +8.6 points of tail per CAGR point over the notch before it, the largest increment on the ladder). The knee is read off the *increment* in the price of return rather than its level, so no threshold had to be chosen.

**6. Does Nasdaq shift the frontier outward?** Yes. On the joint frontier 11 of the 28 Nasdaq cells are robust against 1 of 16 S&P cells, and 6 S&P cells that are efficient within their own family stop being efficient once the Nasdaq cells are in the comparison. At 3 of the 3 return tiers both families reach, the Nasdaq candidate gets there on less option capital, and at 2 of them it is also better on both the fifth percentile and the tail probability. **This is the finding most dependent on an assumption.** The Nasdaq's excess growth over the S&P is an *input* to every resampled path, not a result of any of them, and the volatility premium charged to Nasdaq options is imported unchanged from a less volatile market.

**7. Can Nasdaq achieve S&P 90/50-like growth with only 20-30% option capital?** No, not that one. The nearest Nasdaq cell at a budget of 30% or less is NDX_90_30, 1.16% short of SPX_90_50 on the median 30-year CAGR, outside the 1% band. It does reach SPX_90_40 on the same 30% of capital, but not SPX_90_50 and SPX_95_50.

**8. Does that advantage survive the dot-com bust?** The bust is inside the sample, not excluded, and it is the worst equity episode either family faces. Measured against SPX_90_40, NDX_90_30 lost -53.2% across 2000-2002 where the S&P cell lost -52.7%; against SPX_90_50, NDX_90_30 lost -53.2% across 2000-2002 where the S&P cell lost -66.3%; against SPX_95_50, NDX_90_30 lost -53.2% across 2000-2002 where the S&P cell lost -69.0%. So the episode is not uniformly kind to the Nasdaq structures, and where they lose more it is the price of the advantage they show elsewhere.

**9. Does it survive block-bootstrap reordering?** Against SPX_90_40 the gap is -0.16% and +0.39% on a 0.91% bar; against SPX_90_50 the gap is -1.24% and -1.02% on a 1.08% bar; against SPX_95_50 the gap is -1.10% and -1.07% on a 1.39% bar. It holds against SPX_90_40 and SPX_95_50 and not against SPX_90_50. The whole comparison is already made on reordered history — the primary run is a 63-session moving-block bootstrap — so this asks only whether the block length the reordering uses changes the answer; the two figures quoted are 21 sessions and 126.

**10. Does it survive +6 vol-point option pricing?** Against SPX_90_40 the gap is +1.05% on a 0.91% bar; against SPX_90_50 the gap is +0.17% on a 1.08% bar; against SPX_95_50 the gap is +0.75% on a 1.39% bar. It holds against SPX_90_40, SPX_90_50 and SPX_95_50 — every target. The Nasdaq structures are charged the S&P's loading in absolute volatility points, which on a more volatile underlying is proportionally a smaller charge, so a dearer option arm costs the higher-budget S&P cells more than it costs these; that is why the gap moves the way it does. This arm is the closest thing here to a correction for the imported premium, and it is not a measurement of real Nasdaq option prices.

**11. Which strategies remain efficient across most sensitivities?** SPX_80_25, NDX_80_20, NDX_80_25, NDX_80_30, NDX_80_35, NDX_80_40, NDX_80_45, NDX_85_25, NDX_85_30, NDX_85_35, NDX_90_35 and NDX_90_40. These are the cells that are non-dominated in the primary specification and in at least 5 of the 7 sensitivities.

**12. Which apparent winners are conditional on favourable assumptions?** NDX_90_50 (holds 2 of 7, failing block_21:baseline, iv_premium:iv_0, iv_premium:iv_6, roll_interval:roll_18m, roll_interval:roll_9m), NDX_85_45 (holds 3 of 7, failing block_21:baseline, iv_premium:iv_0, iv_premium:iv_6, roll_interval:roll_18m), NDX_90_45 (holds 3 of 7, failing block_21:baseline, iv_premium:iv_0, iv_premium:iv_6, roll_interval:roll_18m) and NDX_80_50 (holds 4 of 7, failing block_126:baseline, iv_premium:iv_0, roll_interval:roll_9m).

**13. Does 1.25x Treasury leverage change any important ranking?** On the same 3,000 worlds, a quarter-turn of duration moves median CAGR by +0.31% on average and P(DD>60%) by +2.9%, and the rank correlation of median CAGR across the 44 cells is 0.9996. 6 of the 44 cells change frontier membership (NDX_80_20, NDX_80_50, NDX_85_20, NDX_85_25, NDX_85_50 and NDX_90_30). The cells keep their order by return, but membership at the boundary is not stable to it, so the sleeve is a dial that changes which cells are called efficient without changing which earn more.

**14. Which 3-5 portfolios represent genuinely distinct risk regimes?** **conservative growth** — SPX_80_25 (12.00% median, 6.94% p5, -36.3% median drawdown), **balanced growth** — NDX_80_25 (13.74% median, 7.32% p5, -41.0% median drawdown), **enhanced growth** — NDX_85_30 (15.37% median, 7.46% p5, -48.1% median drawdown), **aggressive growth** — NDX_90_35 (16.77% median, 7.20% p5, -56.0% median drawdown) and **maximum growth** — NDX_90_40 (17.66% median, 6.96% p5, -61.6% median drawdown). Each holds at least 5 of 7 sensitivities and is separated from the one below it on at least 2 of the four frontier axes beyond the tolerances; the separation walk left 8 such cells and the menu is the 5 of them spread most evenly along the risk axis, both ends kept.

## What would change these answers

Nothing here observes an option price. The volatility every structure is charged
is a trailing realized proxy plus a flat loading, and the sensitivity arms move
that loading by three points in each direction because three points is a plausible
error, not because it is a measured one. A real skew surface would change the
level of every number above and could change the ordering of any pair whose gap
is inside its resolution bar — which, given the size of the volatility unit at the
expensive end of the lattice, is most adjacent pairs.

The bootstrap resamples one history. It cannot produce a crash worse than the
ones in the record, a bond regime unlike the one observed, or a Nasdaq that did
not out-grow the S&P over this window. That growth gap is an input to every path,
so no amount of resampling can test it, and it is the single assumption the
cross-underlying result rests on.
